A GUIDE FOR BEGINNERS

From Information to the Right Visual

Choosing a visual is not just about appearance. First, identify what your audience needs to understand: a difference, trend, sequence, relationship, schedule or structure.

1. Understand Your Information

Start with numbers, dates, steps, choices, relationships or ideas.

2. Identify Your Message

Decide whether to communicate comparison, change, order, progress or structure.

3. Select the Visual

Choose a chart or diagram that communicates the message accurately and clearly.

The book introduces guidance for choosing among 112 coded visual families, including numerical and non-numerical formats.

PRACTICAL EXAMPLES

Two Ways to Turn Information into a Visual

Example 1: Compare Categories

Suppose you have three categories with different values:

CategoryValue
A10
B30
C20

Suitable visual: Column Chart.

Why? The main purpose is to compare values across categories.

Example 2: Explain a Workflow

Suppose you want to describe a process with three stages:

Collect Data → Analyze Data → Present Results

Suitable visual: Process Flowchart.

Why? The main purpose is to communicate the order of steps, not numerical values.

Both example inputs have been tested using the companion Visual Builder. They demonstrate how different types of information call for different visual formats.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Questions Beginners Ask

What chart should I use to compare categories?

A bar chart or column chart is often appropriate when your goal is to compare values across distinct categories.

What is the difference between a timeline and a Gantt chart?

A timeline communicates the order of events or milestones. A Gantt chart shows tasks scheduled across time, often including their durations and overlap.

Can I create useful visuals without numerical data?

Yes. Flowcharts, mind maps, organizational charts, roadmaps and relationship diagrams communicate processes, concepts and structures.

Is From Zero to Visual suitable for beginners?

The book is written for readers who want to learn how to select an appropriate visual before using design tools. It includes practical examples and an interactive companion toolkit.
